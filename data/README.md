# Question set (data card)

- **What:** 120 original English short-answer factual questions; 4 domains (science, geography, history, general knowledge) × 30; tiers easy/medium/hard = 11/12/7 per domain.
- **Fields (`questions.jsonl`):** `question_id`, `domain`, `tier`, `question`, `answer` (gold), `aliases` (accepted surface forms), `normalized_aliases`, `source_title`, `source_url` (Wikipedia, revision-pinned with `oldid`), `source_revision_timestamp`, `evidence_keywords`, `evidence_sentence`, `status`.
- **Verification:** automatic sentence-level evidence check (`src/verify_questions.py`), 120/120 found on 24 Nov 2026. Every item was then reviewed by the AI assistant (3 alias fixes before the run; notes in `ai_review.csv`). Gold answers were verified by this script and by the AI assistant only; there was no human verification. The `ai_verification` field in `verification_report.csv` still carries the original log phrase "verified by AI assistant, pending human spot-check"; that phrase is historical. The `human_verified(Y/N)` column is empty because no human check was performed. That is a limitation of the study, not a step still scheduled.
- **Editing:** change `question_bank_authored.psv` (pipe-separated; see its header line), then re-run the verifier. Don't hand-edit `questions.jsonl`.
- **Pre-registered aliases for G24 (Nur-Sultan / Astana).** The gold answer for "What is the capital city of Kazakhstan?" is Astana. Both `Astana` and `Nur-Sultan` were accepted aliases in the as-run answer key (the city was named Nur-Sultan from 2019 to 2022 and then reverted to Astana). This alias was **not** added after the run. A robustness check treats "Nur-Sultan" as wrong; see Table 5 of the paper.
- **Alias revision v2 (25 Nov 2026, after the model run):** the grader audit added three aliases only:
  - **H29** `Trygve Halvdan Lie` (full name of the first UN Secretary-General; gold: Trygve Lie).
  - **S22** `John Murray`. The question "Who published *On the Origin of Species* in 1859?" is ambiguous between the author (Charles Darwin, the intended gold) and the publisher (John Murray). Both readings are accepted in v2. Reword this item in any future version.
  - **K09** `204`. The question "How many squares are there on a standard chessboard?" is ambiguous between the 64 squares of the grid and the well-known puzzle count of squares of all sizes (204). Both readings are accepted in v2. Reword this item in any future version.
  Question text and sources are unchanged. `questions.jsonl` was patched in place from the bank (pinned revisions kept, field `alias_revision` added) rather than re-fetched. The exact file used for the run is kept as `questions_v1_as_run.jsonl` (SHA-256 88b7882986da0a09af9e3e227f0b45896449167ad0647fb6bb1dfa0ea38b1647); the v2 file has SHA-256 bf70fd3d8870c85a97364e17e7eae771815174ebf29e6a6355d9bbaa0b12f21d.

A few evidence sentences are indirect support (S02, S30, G27) or use the 2-sentence window (S22, G03, G19). These items were part of the script and AI-assistant review; they were not later checked by a human.
